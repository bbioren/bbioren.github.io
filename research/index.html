Hidden States, Open Secrets: Recognizing VLM Failures During Visual Prefill

Kai Hidajat, Zayeed Saffat, Ben Bioren, Zixian Ma, Ranjay Krishna · Under review at ICLR 2027

Vision-language models sometimes answer wrong even when the right visual information is still in their hidden states. We look at visual prefill, the image-token states formed before the model has even seen the question, and ask whether a failure is already visible there. Collecting the states that usually lead to correct answers gives a reference for what good visual representations look like, and we measure how far a new input lands from its nearest reference neighbors.

  • On ScienceQA and ChartQA, inputs the model usually gets wrong sit clearly farther, in angle, from the correct references.
  • On POPE and CV-Bench the separation is much weaker.
  • The pattern holds across different vision architectures and shows up within the first quarter of the visual-token positions.

Large Language Models for Predicting Overall Survival in NSCLC Using Pathology Reports

S. Cui, B. Bioren, J. Hellerstein, F. Yaseen, J. Kang, Y. He, S. R. Bowen, J. Fu · ASTRO 2026 Annual Meeting, poster

Can the free-text pathology report alone predict overall survival for non-small cell lung cancer? We compared a zero-shot LLM against text embeddings, with and without fine-tuning.

  • Zero-shot Gemini reached an AUC of 0.641 (95% CI 0.524–0.757).
  • Pre-trained Jina-V3 embeddings reached 0.615, and fine-tuning them raised it to 0.675 (95% CI 0.558–0.793).
  • The differences between the models were not statistically significant.

Minimal Perturbation Attacks in VLMs: Turning Embedding Distance into an Attack Metric

Allen School, with Justin Zhou · 2026 · in progress

targeting ICML 2027

How close an attacked image sits to the text in embedding space predicts whether a typographic attack works. So can you skip the visible text and just optimize the pixels toward that embedding? So far: PGD matches the embedding almost perfectly (cosine similarity 0.988 or more) and OCR stops seeing any text (77.3% → 6.0% detected), but the model mostly ignores it, 31.3% success against 98.0% for visible text. Figuring out what the model reads that the embedding doesn't capture is the next step. The photo on my home page is a toy version of this.

Lean that reads like math

Math AI Lab · January 2026 · in progress

An abstraction layer over Lean 4 so that frontier LLMs can write proofs in notation a working mathematician would recognize, with grammar-constrained decoding and type-aware beam search to keep the output valid. With graduate mathematicians it cut formalization time by about 3x. I'm also formalizing chapter 2 of Boyd's Convex Optimization.
